1. The Origins & The Async Institute (The Backstory)
Ivan Beck (The Architect): Born in 1931, Beck lost his childhood home in a 1937 flood. Decades later, while testing a model fusion reactor in 1972, a solar flare caused an overload that gave him a glimpse into "The Complex." Crucially, the Backrooms began building itself out of Ivan's own memories (which explains the 90s office aesthetics and specific paintings).

Project KV31: Beck forms the Async Research Institute in San Jose, California. By October 17, 1989, Async successfully opens "The Threshold"—the first permanent doorway into the Backrooms.

The Earthquake: The sheer force of tearing open reality to open The Threshold triggered the historical 1989 Loma Prieta earthquake in the real world.

The Goal: Async partners with the Department of Energy to commercialize the Backrooms, intending to solve world overpopulation by selling the infinite space for residential and commercial storage.

2. The Complex & Null Zones (Level Design Mechanics)
Null Zones (Spawning/Portals): These are invisible tears in electromagnetic fields scattered across the real world. Civilians frequently fall through them (noclip) into the Backrooms. Falling through a Null Zone often causes temporal distortion (e.g., researcher Peter Tench fell through a Null Zone and time-traveled two months into the future).

The Green Light (Map Expansion): The Backrooms isn't entirely static; it actively generates new terrain. This "expansion" event is signaled by a massive flash of green light, where walls warp and new spaces (neighborhoods, pools, random furniture) are instantly forged from human memories.

Biomes/Levels: The traditional yellow wallpaper is just the surface (Subdivision A). Deeper explorations reveal "Pitfalls" (a street of houses bathed in red light), dark industrial spaces, fake forests, and makeshift radio towers.

3. The Entities (Enemy Types)
If you are coding monster AI, the lore provides two distinct types of hostiles:

The Bacteria / Life Forms: When a human dies in the Backrooms, a mutated strain of Haybacillus bacteria overtakes their corpse. It stunts decomposition and strings their vocal cords and limbs together. These monsters mimic human speech (like crying out for help or repeating panicked phrases) to lure in new prey. They are fast, aggressive, and nest in dark zones.

The Still Lifes: These are bizarre, distorted copies of real human beings or concepts created directly by the Complex itself. For example, a man named Clark finds a monstrous, oversized copy of himself ("Captain Clark") that hunts him down. They can possess primitive intelligence, turning off light switches and knocking on doors to stalk victims.

4. The Human Element & Async Cover-ups (Factions/NPCs)
Outposts & Tape Recorders: Async sets up motion-sensor cameras, wooden walkways, and tape recorders across the early levels.

The Peter Tench Incident: A researcher who time-traveled via a Null Zone went insane upon return, believing Async was an illusion created by the Backrooms. He stole a shotgun, shot a colleague, and escaped, forcing Async to stage a massive cover-up and fake his death.

Lost Civilians: Scattered throughout the deeper levels are backpacks, abandoned camcorders, crashed cars, and people driven to madness by isolation and the lack of a sky.
